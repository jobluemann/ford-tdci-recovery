Ford Kuga 2.0 TDCi — DPF Differential Pressure Sensor: Where It Fails

Schematic layout (not to scale). The sensor sits in the engine bay near the battery box; two hoses run down to metal pipes either side of the DPF under the car.

Failure points, in order of likelihood

  1. 1 Split / chafed rubber hoses — the two small hoses crack with heat and age, or chafe where they route down to the subframe. A split HI-side hose makes the sensor read ~0 kPa and the ECM flags sensor range/performance (P2453).
  2. 2 Soot-blocked hose or metal take-off pipe — the nipple welded to the exhaust clogs with soot. Remove the hose and blow through / clear the pipe with a stiff wire.
  3. 3 Broken wire inside the loom — peel back the tape near the sensor plug; a fatigued conductor (often a blue wire with a tracer stripe) breaks inside intact-looking insulation. Check continuity, not just looks.
  4. 4 Failed sensor — only after 1–3 are ruled out. Verify: ~0–3 kPa at idle, rising smoothly with RPM/load; a stuck or dead reading with clear hoses = replace with an OE-grade sensor.
  5. 5 Genuinely blocked DPF — high ΔP confirmed on live data (e.g. >10–15 kPa at cruise) with healthy hoses/wiring/sensor. Try a forced regeneration (FORScan) or professional cleaning before replacing the filter.

After any repair: clear codes and run "Reset Differential Pressure Sensor Learned Values" and "Reset DPF Learned Values" in FORScan — this is a legitimate service procedure, not an override.
